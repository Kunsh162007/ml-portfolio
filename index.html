Project 1 of 6 Open source
Open sourceSeptember 2026

Margin

An offline study assistant that reads your textbooks and past papers, finds what the exam actually asks, and turns it into notes and practice questions.

It reads PDFs, Word files, slides and photos of pages, including equations drawn as images. It ranks chapters by how many marks past papers give them, then spends its effort there.

Every sentence in its notes is checked against the book and removed if the book does not support it. Practice answers are marked point by point, and questions you miss come back on a spaced schedule.

It all runs on your own computer with a small local model, with no account or internet after setup. I chose the model by benchmarking nine open models on CPU and GPU.

DRISHTI

A crime-analytics platform that turns police FIR records into decision support for Karnataka State Police.

It finds statistical crime hotspots and emerging trends, maps suspect and call-record networks, and traces money through cybercrime cases.

Its assistant answers only from records it retrieved, cites the FIRs it used, and refuses when nothing matches. I led a team of four; the live build runs on synthetic data, and we designed the architecture for a real pilot on PostGIS, Neo4j and Qdrant.

Project 3 of 6 Live demo
Live demoJune 2026

Agentic Research

A research agent that searches the web, arXiv and GitHub, grades its own sources and draft, and keeps going until the answer is good enough.

It follows the Self-RAG pattern. Each round it decides whether it needs more information, keeps only relevant results, drafts an answer and scores it from 0 to 100. When the score reaches the target it writes a cited report.

Every step is saved, so a crashed run picks up where it stopped. A daily monitor tracks the topics you follow and surfaces new papers.

Project 4 of 6 Medical imaging
Medical imagingMarch 2026

Alzheimer's MRI

Five convolutional networks that grade Alzheimer's disease from brain scans, and six ways of showing where each one looked.

I trained a custom CNN, AlexNet, VGG16, GoogLeNet and ResNet50, rebuilt two of them in PyTorch to compare frameworks, and combined them into an ensemble.

Grad-CAM, Guided Backprop, SHAP, LIME, Occlusion and Score-CAM check that the models focus on the ventricles and cortex, where the disease shows up.

Project 5 of 6 Time series
Time seriesApril 2026

Forecasting

Seven recurrent networks trained on the same Melbourne temperature data, from a plain RNN up to an encoder–decoder with attention.

Given 30 days of history, each model predicts the days ahead. Every model uses the same data and the same test split, so the comparison is fair.

I wrote a Bahdanau attention layer and a tool that shows gradients fading through time, which is why plain RNNs struggle on long sequences.
